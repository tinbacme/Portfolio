# Who Tin is: interview notes (Nov 8, 2026)

Answers from a one-question-at-a-time interview, used to shape the homepage direction. Quotes are Tin's own words, lightly trimmed.

## Background

- Born in Vietnam, moved to the US between ages 8 and 12.
- Grew up in **Đà Lạt**. The family had two homes: one inside the market, one on farmland.
- Memories: "having to drive through the rain with my dad. It was super cold, but also gloomy and warming." Cool morning drives through hills and trees. Lots of coffee shops. Fog and pine hills. "It's very similar to Portland, actually."
- Studied industrial design at the University of Oregon: pine and fog again.

## What Tin loves

- Making physical things: lamps, furniture, mechanisms, 3D printing, woodworking. "I love the mechanics side of things."
- Work that is "both technical, but then also visually beautiful and also for a lot of people."
- Small changes with huge reach: "I reached billions of people by just changing a certain design" (Windows Search).
- Hobbies now: designing, making, drawing, building websites and apps.
- No single signature object. **Matcha is not core**: drop it from the site.

## Style

- Minimal: Dieter Rams / Braun, Eames / mid-century US, Muji / Naoto Fukasawa.
- A hint of Vietnamese culture and a hint of American culture, plus Japanese restraint and 20th-century design.
- Avoid: dark tech / neon (black backgrounds, glow, glass, sci-fi type). Tin did not rule out 3D, illustration or Vietnamese motifs, but tourist clichés should still be handled with care.

## What the site must do

- First feeling for a hiring manager: **curious maker**.
- "Simple but delightful." Brings people joy. Shows "I really care about making people's lives easier and better through design."
- Easter egg: **a tiny thing Tin built**, a real mini app or tool hidden in the site that people can actually use. Goal: "Oh, that's kind of cool. I didn't know Tin could do that."

## Voice

**Plain and warm.** Simple sentences, friendly, no jargon. Sample:

> Hi, I'm Tin. I design products that make everyday things a little easier, like the search box on your Windows PC. I grew up in Đà Lạt, studied industrial design in Oregon, and I still like building things with my hands.

## Possible core idea

The drive between two homes: cold, foggy and gloomy outside, warm inside. Đà Lạt and Portland share the same pine and fog. Design that makes life easier is the warm room at the end of the drive.
