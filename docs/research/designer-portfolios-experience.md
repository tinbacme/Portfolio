# How top product/UX designers present experience on their portfolio sites

Research date: 2026-11-16. Purpose: pick a pattern for the owner's "Experience" section (Senior Product Designer, Microsoft AI; product design instructor, University of Oregon; ex-Salesforce; ex-2U bootcamp instructor; BS Industrial Design).

## Method and limits

- 43 personal sites were attempted and 41 were examined. Two failed (mercedesbazan.com and jordansinger.com returned nothing usable). One more, femke.design, turned out to be a course business, so it is listed but left out of the tally. That leaves **40 sites in the tally**.
- **Direct page fetching was blocked.** This cloud session's network allowlist would not resolve rauno.me, emilkowal.ski, brianlovin.com, paco.me, jordansinger.com or karrisaarinen.com, so I stopped trying direct fetches. Each site was examined through the search engine's index of its own pages instead: site-restricted searches that return the rendered text of the homepage, About, Work or Résumé pages. That means **structure, wording, ordering and dates are verified, but visual details (alignment, hover previews, motion, typography) mostly are not.** Where a visual detail is given, it comes from the indexed text structure (for example "Figma 2026– Designer").
- Some indexed snapshots are a few months old, and several sites had older and newer versions in the index. The current version is described wherever the index made it clear which that was.

Where it lives: on the **homepage for about 55%** (mostly as a single line or a tiny ledger), and on **About/Work for about 40%**. **None of the 40 uses a visual timeline graphic, a skill bar, or a wall of employer logos.** Only Bilotta uses cards. Explicit LinkedIn hand-offs appear (Bleasel), but most sites simply put a LinkedIn icon in the footer.

## The 7 strongest patterns

1. **Compact year ledger, one row per company.** Company · title(s) · years. Newest first, no bullets.
   Examples: https://daneden.me, https://www.davidhoang.com/about, https://brianlovin.com/about
   Eden's trick: promotions inside one company are stacked as titles in one row ("Product Designer, Design Lead, Design Manager").
2. **Ledger + one-line description per row.** Same as 1, plus a single sentence on scope or outcome.
   Examples: https://haydenbleasel.com/about, https://www.davidhoang.com/works, https://cv.shud.in
3. **"Currently / previously" line on the homepage.** Two sentences at most, and the work does the rest.
   Examples: https://rauno.me, https://nelson.co, https://paco.me
4. **Multi-hat stacked line.** Short parallel sentences for concurrent roles (job, book, teaching, community).
   Examples: https://jonyablonski.com, https://www.odannyboy.com, https://eleganthack.com/about/
5. **Year-tagged index of work.** Jobs and projects in one list with years on the right, and gaps or overlaps shown honestly ("Apple 2018, 2021–2023").
   Examples: https://jasonyuan.design, https://rog.ie, https://anthonyhobday.com
6. **Narrative list in the designer's voice.** Verb-led lines that turn a non-linear path (industrial design → product → teaching) into a story.
   Examples: https://tarng.com, https://benji.org
7. **Two depths: short on the page, full CV one click away** (PDF or subpage).
   Examples: https://jonyablonski.com/info/ ("Download Resume"), https://simonpan.com, https://shud.in → cv.shud.in

## What top designers consistently do

- **Keep it very short.** The median is 0–6 rows. Even Hoang, with 20 years of experience, shows a terse list, and Bleasel stops and points to LinkedIn for older roles.
- **Put the current role first,** usually as a sentence ("Currently…", "Most recently…").
- **Use years, not months,** in the compact versions ("2018–22", "2024–"). Month-year ranges show up only on full CV-style pages (Bleasel, Bryn Taylor, Lynn).
- **Use an open range for the current role** ("2024–", "2026–", "present").
- **Describe what they built, not what they were responsible for.** Paco: "developed the Vercel design system, website, and dashboard". Hoang: "led the rebrand…".
- **Attach years to shipped work rather than jobs** when the work is the point (Rogie, Nelson, Yuan).
- **Name the company and leave the rest out** when the work is confidential. Widua (Apple), Yuan (Apple) and Jenny Wen (Anthropic) give the role and a one-line scope, with no screenshots and no metrics, and let side projects or older case studies carry the visual proof.
- **Give teaching and writing equal billing** with the day job when they matter (Yablonski, Saffer, Wodtke, Hobday, Tarng's "Elsewhere").
- **Link out for the full record:** a PDF, a CV subpage, or LinkedIn in the footer.

## What they avoid

- Bulleted responsibility lists ("Collaborated with cross-functional partners…"). None of the 40 top-tier sites do this.
- Skill bars, tool-logo grids, "years of experience" counters (only a couple of generic templates do this).
- Employer logo walls (client lists appear only on freelancer/studio sites).
- Timeline graphics with dots and connecting lines.
- Invented or unbacked metrics. Metrics appear only inside case studies (Simon Pan's Uber pickup work), never in the experience list.
- Listing every early job. Older roles get collapsed or delegated.

## Recommendations for the owner

Context: concurrent roles (Microsoft AI design + UO teaching), an NDA on recent Microsoft work, earlier industry (Salesforce) and teaching (2U) roles, and an industrial design degree.

1. **Hero line (multi-hat, pattern 4):** "Senior Product Designer at Microsoft AI. I also teach product design at the University of Oregon." Two parallel sentences, like Yablonski or Saffer. That settles the overlap before anyone reaches the list.
2. **Experience as a compact ledger (pattern 1/2), split into two short groups** so the overlapping dates don't look like a scheduling conflict:
   - **Design:** Microsoft AI, Senior Product Designer, 20XX– · Salesforce, title(s), 20XX–XX · (earlier)
   - **Teaching:** University of Oregon, Instructor/Adjunct, Product Design, 20XX– · 2U, Bootcamp Instructor (UX/UI), 20XX–XX

   A single list with a small "Teaching" tag also works (Hobday puts teaching in the same ledger), but two labelled groups read more clearly when dates overlap. Years go right-aligned in tabular numerals, the current rows have open ranges, past rows are muted.
3. **Salesforce promotions:** use Eden's stacked-titles row (one company, titles in sequence) instead of several rows.
4. **NDA handling:** give the Microsoft row a one-line scope with no screens and no numbers, in the style of Jenny Wen, Widua or Nelson. For example: "Designing AI experiences for [product area]. Work under NDA. Happy to walk through it in conversation." Tie the Salesforce and teaching rows to the case studies that can be shown, and let those carry the visual proof.
5. **Teaching row content:** something concrete and non-confidential, such as course names, cohorts or the number of students taught, or a line on approach. Wodtke and Saffer show that academic roles read as seniority, not a side gig.
6. **Industrial design origin:** one closing line, in Tarng's style: "Studied Industrial Design (BS) — still think in materials and constraints." It is a differentiator, and none of the ledgers above do this well.
7. **Depth on demand (pattern 7):** add "Résumé (PDF)" and LinkedIn links under the ledger, so older roles and month-level dates live there, not on the page.
8. **Keep it short:** 4–6 rows in all, no bullets, no logos, no timeline graphic, no skill bars.
