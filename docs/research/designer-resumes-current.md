# How currently employed product designers present their résumés

Research date: 18–19 Nov 2026. Purpose: inform a rewrite of a one-page résumé for a Senior Product Designer at Microsoft AI (Bing / Edge / Windows search; previously Salesforce intern → Associate Designer → Designer, 2017–2022; part-time product design teaching at a university since 2023 and at a 2U-run UX/UI bootcamp since 2021; BS Industrial Design).

## Method and limits

- Public pages only. Nothing required a login. Each résumé PDF was downloaded and its text was extracted with `pdftotext`. Layouts were checked by rendering page 1 to an image.
- **Who counts as "current":** the designer's own résumé or site, dated 2025–2026, shows a role marked "Present", "Now" or "Currently at X".
- **Left out:** job seekers, template and example-résumé sites, résumé-writing services and generated career-blog examples.
- **Sample size:** 17 people were verified as currently employed in design (or design-research leadership). For 11 of them I analyzed the full résumé document. For the other 6, only the experience list on their portfolio site was available, so they count toward conventions (NDA handling, teaching, title lines) but not toward the résumé-document tallies.
- **Gaps:**
  - read.cv no longer serves profiles. `read.cv/<user>` returns `402 DEPLOYMENT_DISABLED`.
  - Peerlist résumé pages are behind a bot check.
  - Several Dropbox-hosted résumés could not be reached from this environment.
  - Public résumés from *current* Salesforce designers were scarce. The one current Salesforce lead found gates all work behind a passcode and posts no résumé.
  - No verified designer in the sample lists a formal adjunct university title. The teaching evidence comes from bootcamp mentors, instructors, guest lecturers and TAs (see §3).
- Job-posting statements below come from search-result summaries of aggregator pages. They are not official Microsoft pages.

### Other things these designers do that a plain résumé misses
1. A company or team descriptor line, so a reader knows what "Apps admin center" or "Entra" is (Freed, Singh, Gansallo).
2. Building and coding signals: "Shipped Production Pull Requests" (Roy); "Built internal AI design tooling … Figma plugin" (Freed); "production-like React prototyping environment" (Gansallo).
3. Bolded outcomes inside bullets for scanning (Roy).
4. Older roles cut hard to keep one page (Iqbal; Roy's internship sidebar).
5. A .docx or web-text version next to the designed PDF (Roy; Freed's HTML résumé).

## 3. Recommendations for this résumé

These follow from the patterns above. Placeholders in [brackets] are for the owner to fill in. None of this is invented.

1. **Headline:** use one line under the name in the "Title | Specialty" form used by 3 of the 6 headlines (Hemphill, Singh, Gansallo). For example: **"Senior Product Designer | Search & AI Experiences"**. Put the portfolio URL (with "password on request", or the password itself as Iqbal does) and LinkedIn on the contact line, as 10 / 11 do.
2. **Summary:** yes (10 / 11 have one). Keep it to 2–3 lines. Open with title + years + domain, and name the current employer and surfaces, as Roy and Singh do. For example: *"Senior Product Designer at Microsoft AI with [N]+ years designing search and AI experiences across Bing, Edge and Windows. Previously [N] years at Salesforce. I also teach product design at [university] and [bootcamp]."* Mention teaching in the summary only if it supports the target role; Rezende does this with "teach other designers to do the same".
3. **Microsoft entry:** one company heading. Title on the left and "Mon YYYY – Present" on the right. Add a product/team line such as "Microsoft AI — Bing, Edge & Windows search" (7 / 11 use a sub-line). Then **3–5 bullets** (median current-role count is 3; strong résumés reach 6–7 when the role is the main story).
4. **Salesforce:** **one "Salesforce" heading with the three titles stacked under it**, each with its own dates: Designer [dates], Associate Designer [dates], Intern [dates].
   - The evidence is split: 4 list separately, 2 group.
   - Grouping is the better fit here. It shows the promotions at a glance, saves the lines that a repeated company name would use on a one-page résumé, and is how Z. Ahmed (also ex-Microsoft Search) and Freed handle multiple titles.
   - Give the top title 2–3 bullets, the Associate title 1–2, and the intern title one line or none, following the shrink-older-roles pattern (Iqbal, Roy, Freed).
   - Give each title its own dated line rather than slash-combining, so an ATS can still parse the titles.
5. **Teaching:** a **separate "Teaching" section** below Experience. The 6 people who show teaching as more than a job bullet all use a separate section, and no one puts it in the main Experience list.
   - Use one line per post: "[Exact title on contract], Product Design — [University], [Mon YYYY] – Present", and the same for the bootcamp.
   - For the bootcamp, use the program name and title exactly as contracted. 2U-run bootcamps carry a university's brand, so name it as the contract does rather than guessing.
   - At most one bullet each (cohort size, courses, curriculum work).
   - **Alternative, only if more weight is wanted:** put the posts in Experience and mark them "Part-time", as Gansallo does for a concurrent role.
6. **Microsoft NDA work:**
   - Follow what every current-Microsoft résumé does: **name the shipped surfaces** (Bing, Edge, Windows search), describe the problem space, your role and the scale, and use **public-scale or relative metrics** ("used by [public figure] monthly users", "improved [metric] by X%") only where they are cleared.
   - Leave out codenames and unreleased features.
   - If a major project is unreleased, a résumé-safe pattern adapted from Vino is a final clause such as "…plus unreleased AI search work (details under NDA)". Use it sparingly: no résumé in the sample says "NDA".
   - Handle confidentiality on the portfolio, either with a password next to the URL (Iqbal) or with "Confidential — available on request" (Rezende).
   - It is normal for the Microsoft section to carry fewer numbers: 4 of 7 Microsoft designers have none there.
7. **Skills/Tools:** add one compact block (10 / 11 have one).
   - Group it in 2–3 lines: Design (interaction, systems, prototyping, research); Tools (Figma, …); AI & code (the AI prototyping tools actually used; 7 / 11 list them).
   - Keep it to keywords. That also helps ATS matching.
8. **Date format:** "Mon YYYY – Present" everywhere (7 / 11), with dates aligned in one column.
9. **Education:** last, on one line: "B.S. Industrial Design, [School], [Year]". Every sample places education at the bottom or in a sidebar. Roy and Naik list industrial design degrees plainly.
10. **Easy extras:**
    - A product line under each title.
    - Bold one outcome per bullet (Roy).
    - An optional single line for recognition or patents if any (6 / 11).
    - A building/shipping signal if true (prototypes in code, plugins, PRs).

## 4. ATS-friendliness vs. designer conventions

| Area | Agree or conflict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Standard headings (Summary, Experience, Skills, Education) | **Agree.** Most samples use them. Unusual labels (Iqbal "Accolades", Singh "Role-relevant expertise", Rezende "Proficiency") are the exceptions. | Records 1–14 |
| Skills / keyword block | **Agree.** 10 / 11 include one, which feeds keyword matching. | §2 table |
| "Mon YYYY – Present" dates, title + company per line | **Agree.** This is the majority practice and also what parsers expect. | §2 table |
| Separate dated line per title (Salesforce promotions) | **Agree when grouped carefully.** One company heading with each title on its own dated line keeps the visual grouping and keeps every title parseable. Avoid slash-combined titles ("UX Group Manager / Senior UX Designer", Freed) or inline lists (Z. Ahmed) if ATS parsing matters. | Records 1, 11 |
| Single column, no tables | **Conflict.** 7 / 11 designers use two or three columns or sidebars. In my own `pdftotext` checks, Freed's sidebar words were mixed into his bullets, Iqbal's custom font came out as garbled text, and even single-column Hemphill had duplicated text layers. Only the single-column, plain-font files (Naik, Ok, Z. Ahmed) and Roy's sidebar file extracted cleanly. | Records 1, 3, 4, 7, 11, 13, 14 |
| Visual craft (large type, theme rails, bold metrics) | **Partial conflict.** Bold inside bullets is harmless. Rails and multi-column grids risk mis-ordered text. | Records 4, 5, 7 |
| Portfolio link on the résumé | **Agree.** ATS keeps the URL, and Microsoft postings reportedly ask that "Resume must include a link to design portfolio" (search summary of a posting at <https://queertech.getro.com/companies/microsoft/jobs/58453876-senior-product-designer>). | §2 table |

**Practical resolution, seen in the sample:** keep **one ATS-safe single-column version** (PDF exported from a real-text layout, plus a .docx, as Roy offers) for applications. Keep a designed version for portfolio and direct sends if wanted. For this résumé the single-column version should come first, because it has to fit on one page and pass a big-tech ATS.
